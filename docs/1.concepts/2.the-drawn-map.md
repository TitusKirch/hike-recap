# The drawn map

There is no map library and there are no map tiles. Everything under the routes is computed from a raster elevation model and an OpenStreetMap extract, then drawn as SVG plus one canvas layer.

## How it works

Layers, bottom to top:

1. **Hillshade** — computed from the DEM into a `<canvas>`. This is what makes mountains read as mountains.
2. **Water** — lake polygons from OSM, as flat dark shapes.
3. **Contours** — marching squares over the DEM grid. Raw segments, not stitched paths: at these stroke widths the result is visually identical and far simpler.
4. **Routes** — the tracks, the current tour glowing in its accent colour, the others dimmed but keeping their own colour.
5. **Labels** — places and peaks, collision-avoided, capped at roughly 20 per view. See [Label placement](4.label-placement.md).

The DEM is a terrarium-encoded raster: `(R × 256 + G + B / 256) − 32768`. `src/util/terrain.ts` turns the grid into hillshade, contour segments and the ground tint; `src/scene/TerrainCanvas.vue` rasterises it once per framed extent, and `src/scene/MapVector.vue` draws everything vector.

**Green is the base colour and rock is the exception.** Drawing only OSM-tagged vegetation looked wrong: alpine pasture is largely untagged, so most of the map fell back to bare cream and read as scree. The ground tone therefore comes from elevation (`hypsometric()`), with tagged woodland painted over it.

**Contour detail follows the zoom.** The DEM is a fixed raster, so a tight frame magnifies it heavily and a coarse pooling factor leaves a close view almost blank. `MapVector` picks the pooling factor and contour interval from `view.scale` — roughly 50 m spacing on a wide frame, 25 m close in, so zooming visibly thickens the terrain.

## Why it's built this way

Every tile provider's free tier either brands the map with a logo or restricts video use. Drawing the map removes the API key, the quota, the logo and the network dependency in one move, and is why `maplibre-gl` is not a dependency. The full trade-off, including what this costs, is [ADR-0003](../99.adr/0003-draw-the-map-instead-of-fetching-tiles.md).

**Attribution is still mandatory, and it is not decoration.** Terrain Tiles mixes its sources by area — SRTM globally, with 3DEP, EU-DEM, Kartverket and others taking over in places — and a downloaded tile does not record which one it came from. `src/util/attribution.ts` therefore emits the two lines that are always true, matches regional duties against a coarse bounding-box table, and appends anything listed under `attribution` in `tours.json`. On an overlap it over-credits by design. A tool that silently drops attribution makes its users infringe.

### What the attribution line actually says

The table is additive, not a lookup: whatever matches, the two always-true lines are there too. A walk in Tirol therefore credits SRTM, OpenStreetMap, Copernicus **and** the Austrian DGM — four lines, all four owed. A region no box covers still gets the two, plus anything `tours.json` declares. Nothing needs configuring for a new region; the boxes live in `src/util/attribution.ts` and are read from the tracks' own bounds.

Two things a reader would not find in the code:

- **ETOPO1 is left out on purpose.** Terrain Tiles credits it, but it supplies ocean bathymetry and this draws land relief for a walked track. On a coastal trip where the sea floor is visibly part of the frame, put `U.S. National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration` in `attribution` and it joins the rest.
- **The strip has to fit, and how many lines it takes is not fixed.** Set on one line it ran off both edges of the frame the moment an area owed a fourth credit. It wraps within the safe area now, and the floor labels may occupy is derived from the wrapped height rather than being a constant — so a region with five obligations pushes the labels up instead of colliding with the text.

If your area's obligation is missing rather than merely uncovered, that is a bug in the table, not something to paper over in `tours.json` — the point of the table is that a user who never thinks about attribution still ships a correct one.

### Three traps the fetch step hides

All three were invisible while only one region existed, and all three fail quietly:

- **Fetch for the extent the camera sees, not the bounding box of the tracks.** The frame is portrait, so a wide, shallow route is fitted to the width and the view then reaches far above and below it. Fetching only the track box leaves bare relief with land cover ending mid-frame.
- **A `View` carries its own zoom.** Building a view at one zoom and projecting at another places everything off-screen, and nothing throws. This is why `zoom` is a field of `View` in `src/util/camera.ts` rather than a parameter threaded through call sites.
- **OSM multipolygons arrive as separate shoreline fragments.** Closing each fragment on its own turns a lake into a fan of triangles; `assembleRings()` joins them by matching endpoints.

Overpass also needs a `User-Agent` — Node's default agent gets a flat 406.
