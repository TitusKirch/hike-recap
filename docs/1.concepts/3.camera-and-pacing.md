# Camera and pacing

The video has no chapters and no per-tour panels. What carries it is the camera: it opens framed on the first tour alone, and before each later tour pulls back just far enough that everything drawn so far still fits. Tours stay once drawn, so the frame widens as the trip accumulates.

## How it works

`src/util/schedule.ts` gives every tour two phases:

| Phase | Camera                      | Track                  |
| :---- | :-------------------------- | :--------------------- |
| zoom  | pulls back to the new frame | nothing is drawn       |
| draw  | holds still                 | the track draws itself |

`planDrawSchedule()` lays those phases across the frame budget: a lead-in the intro card plays over, a zoom before each tour but the first, the rest drawing, and a hold-out for the outro. `src/util/camera.ts` supplies the views — `viewForBounds()` fits an extent, `collapseViews()` prunes the steps worth animating, and `transformBetween()` expresses a zoom as a CSS transform over already-rasterised layers.

`IntroCard` and `OutroCard` sit over the live map rather than on separate slides; the lead-in and hold-out double as their airtime.

## Why it's built this way

**Slots are proportional to track length, never an equal split.** Equal shares make the drawing head sprint through a long tour and crawl along a short one — on the first real trip an 11.4 km tour and a 4.9 km one differed visibly, and it was the single most obvious flaw in that cut. A test pins the resulting speed spread below 2 %.

**The head is positioned by path length, never by vertex index.** `stroke-dashoffset` reveals a fraction of the path's _length_. Picking a vertex by index instead drifts, because Douglas-Peucker leaves vertices dense in bends and sparse on straights — measured against the real tracks that put the moving dot 4.2 % of the route, some 467 m, away from the line's end. `pointAtFraction()` and the dash reveal now read the same geometry, and a test holds them together.

**The camera holds still where a step would barely move it.** A tour lying inside the frame already shown widens it by nothing: five of one trip's eleven steps came out at zoom 1.000 with a shift under 6 px, which reads as a twitch rather than a move. `collapseViews()` reuses the previous view for those, so the step animates nothing, its frames go to drawing instead, and the layers keyed on the framed extent are not rebuilt. Twelve tours needed only seven distinct views.

**The camera may only move between tours.** This is the render-time rule from [the pipeline](1.the-render-pipeline.md), and it is why a zoom is a transform over finished layers rather than a rebuilt view: an earlier variant nudged the view every frame and pushed a full render past ten minutes.

**Layers are rasterised larger than the frame.** A zoom transform both scales and shifts, and without margin the shift pulls the rasterised area off the edge so the background shows through — measured at up to 146 px on the widest camera move. `OVERSCAN` in `src/video.ts` is 1.35 for that reason.

**Nothing here is a per-trip constant.** The tour count lives in `data/tours.json` and nowhere else; the schedule, the viewport sequence and the colour palette all follow from it. See [ADR-0005](../99.adr/0005-derive-every-constant-from-the-data.md).
